# Suggested GTrack training programs

Research date: 16 September 2026. Status: implemented as the version 1 GTrack program catalog.

These are original GTrack examples informed by Sebastian Oreb’s public coaching principles. They are not official, purchased, reproduced, or endorsed Strength System programs. Exact splits, exercises, sets, repetitions, rest periods and progression rules below are our design choices. Public descriptions do not establish the full prescriptions of his paid programs.

Assumption: general adult gym users with standard gym access. Choose one program, not all three concurrently. Individual ability and recovery determine starting loads; these are examples, not an individual assessment. Use familiar, pain-free movements and have a qualified coach check unfamiliar barbell technique.

## What the research supports

- **Technique and load selection come before chasing harder sessions.** Oreb recommends training below absolute capacity and reserving maximal efforts for testing or competition. [Load Selection](https://strengthsystem.com/all-articles/your-program-sucks-part-2/).
- **Progress should be measurable.** His progression article discusses load, reps and sets, while keeping technique, range of motion and equipment consistent. Its example percentages refer to a repeatable multi-set workload, explicitly not a one-rep maximum. [Progressive Overload](https://strengthsystem.com/all-articles/your-program-sucks-part-3/).
- **Beginners need movement foundations.** His beginner offering centres on push, pull, bend and squat, with a stated four-to-eight-week duration depending on experience. The public page does not disclose its entire routine. [Beginner guide](https://strengthsystem.com/beginners-guide-to-lifting-weights/).
- **Training develops over phases.** The official site describes 12–16-week programs, technical feedback and conservative load prescriptions. [Strength System](https://strengthsystem.com/).
- **His public explanations are contextual.** A 2026 interview describes increasing effort over four weeks; his earlier progression article allows a fourth-week deload. Neither should become an unconditional rule to push every user to failure. [Publisher’s interview notes, 46:49](https://podcasts.apple.com/ca/podcast/coach-to-the-strongest-man-alive-on-breaking-plateaus/id6793696383?i=1000779498914), [earlier article](https://strengthsystem.com/all-articles/your-program-sucks-part-3/).
- **Movement balance matters.** The interview publisher’s recap describes horizontal/vertical pressing and pulling, hip- and knee-dominant squatting, hinging and knee flexion. It also describes familiarisation, muscle-building, strength and peaking phases. This is the publisher’s summary; I did not independently review the complete audio. [OneCoach recap](https://one.coach/resources/how-to-get-stronger/).

Our adaptations use broad movement coverage, rather than claiming equal volume in all eight of those categories. In particular, a generic back squat is not automatically a hip-dominant squat. The starter catalog does not yet distinguish coached squat variations. No mandatory maximal singles or universal percentage-based weight calculator are included.

## How to read and run these examples

All prescriptions are **working sets × reps**. Warm-ups are additional: start light and use progressively heavier, non-fatiguing preparation sets for the main lift. Exercise order is listed in training order. Allow a day between full-body sessions, and follow the spacing suggested for the four-day programs.

**RIR means reps in reserve:** an estimate of how many more clean repetitions you could perform. Three RIR means stopping with about three good reps left. It is approximate, especially for beginners. If technique deteriorates, stop the set even if the target rep count is not reached.

**Starting loads:** choose a weight that permits all sets at the required RIR. Do not enter a universal starting kilogram value. For unfamiliar exercises, begin light and establish a repeatable baseline. Dumbbells use the weight of one dumbbell, barbells include the bar, and unilateral reps are per side, consistent with the library descriptions.

**Rest:** primary and technique-practice exercises: 3–5 minutes; secondary exercises: 2–3 minutes; accessories: 1–2 minutes. Use the longer end when needed to repeat good reps. These are GTrack defaults, not a claim that Oreb prescribes the same rest for every lifter.

**Progression within a phase:** when all prescribed sets are completed with consistent technique and at least the target RIR, add the smallest practical load increment at the next exposure. For example, 1–2.5 kg total for a barbell upper-body lift or 2.5–5 kg for a lower-body lift may be practical; use less if needed. These are options, not compulsory weekly increases. If the next dumbbell or machine jump is too large, repeat the load, or add one rep per set up to two above target before increasing weight and returning to target reps. Do not simultaneously increase load, reps and sets beyond the prescribed phase.

**Missed targets:** repeat or reduce the load next time; do not add weight after missed reps. After two poor exposures, or accumulating fatigue, take an easier week: roughly half the normal sets (round up), lighter weights, at least four RIR. Resume at the last successful workload. If a session is missed, continue the sequence without cramming two sessions together.

**Phase changes:** when reps or set counts change, select the new load from the target effort again. Lower reps do not automatically authorise a large weight jump. The four-day programs use three build weeks followed by an easier week as a simple default; this is our recovery choice, not a universal Oreb rule. For a “3 → 2” RIR phase, use about three in its first week, two to three in the second, and two in the third.

**Final-week review:** compare the first primary set with an earlier set at the same reps and estimated RIR. More load at comparable technique/effort is a useful signal; it is not a tested 1RM. Reduce weight on remaining sets to reach the stated easy effort. Do not force a personal best. Foundation users simply consolidate and choose their next block.

|---|---:|---:|---|

The following session tables show the **first phase**. Subsequent phase tables replace each exercise’s prescription according to its role; they are part of the program, not optional add-on volume.

## Substitutions

Choose one variation for the block and track it consistently. A replacement gets its own baseline weight; do not carry kilograms across different machines or movement types.

| If the listed exercise does not suit equipment or skill | Suggested alternative |
|---|---|
| Barbell squat | Goblet squat or leg press; this changes the barbell-specific goal |
| Trap bar deadlift | Dumbbell Romanian deadlift, with a newly selected working load |
| Barbell bench press | Dumbbell bench press or machine chest press |
| Barbell overhead press | Dumbbell shoulder press or machine shoulder press |
| Seated cable row | Chest-supported dumbbell row or machine row |
| Bulgarian split squat | Dumbbell step-up, reps per leg |
| Seated leg curl | Lying leg curl |

## How these should fit GTrack

Offer a **Programs** catalog with “Preview program” and “Use this program.” Preview should show who it suits, days/week, duration, required equipment, the session sequence and progression. Users choose one and copy its sessions into their own workspace; their edits should not change the shared example.

A **program** is an ordered set of sessions plus week/phase rules. A **workout template** is one reusable session. A **recorded session** is the actual performance. We should keep those concepts separate so a four-day program does not look like four unrelated workouts.

All 11 proposed sessions use names already in the 64-exercise library. The companion JSON is the version 1 application catalog, not a backup import file. It deliberately leaves starting weight unset; users select it before recording. Preserve version 1 prescriptions for existing enrollments when adding future catalog versions.

Implementation notes and remaining refinements:

- Program enrollments sync privately. Completed session metadata identifies enrollment/week/day; unique completed slots determine the next session. Pausing preserves all progress.
- Phase targets and effort guidance appear in preview, session preparation and recording.
- Program exercises have individual rest defaults and visible rest ranges. Added exercises use the session rest setting.
- Working weights are required before starting. Earlier recorded weights are references that the user confirms; there are no automatic load increases.
- Users can add/remove/reorder exercises during a session. Such changes apply to that recorded session; persistent personal program-template substitutions remain a future refinement.
- Log working sets for program adherence; separate warm-up tracking remains a future refinement.
- Describe these as GTrack examples informed by public principles. Link to the research; do not imply Sebastian Oreb endorsement.

The Programs catalog includes Foundation, Strength and Size, and Barbell Strength. Enrollment is opt-in. Partial sessions are saved without advancing by default; the user can explicitly mark them complete. Existing standalone workouts remain available.
